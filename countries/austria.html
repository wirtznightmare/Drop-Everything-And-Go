Austria: the country guide

Imperial palaces in Vienna, Mozart's home town, a lake village that looks like a painting, and some of the best skiing in Europe. Here's when to go, what to do and what to watch out for.

In this guide

  5. Where to go in Austria
  6. Skiing in Austria

Austria's a small country, but it fits a lot in. Vienna has the palaces, the big museums and coffee houses that have barely changed in a century. A couple of hours west on the train you've got Salzburg, with Mozart, baroque churches and the Alps right on its doorstep (I spent five days there in March, and it's somewhere I could genuinely see myself living). Then there's Hallstatt, a village on a mountain lake so ridiculously pretty that it's UNESCO-listed and China has built its own copy. You can do all three in a week and still feel like you've only scratched the surface.

Timing

When to go

Summer · June to September

Best for sightseeing. Long days, warm weather, and every lake, trail and terrace is open. Hallstatt looks its best, but it's also at its busiest, so book somewhere to stay weeks ahead in July and August.

Winter · December to March

Best for atmosphere. The Christmas markets in Vienna and Salzburg are worth the trip on their own. The skiing starts in December, and Hallstatt under snow is beautiful, with fewer people about.

Shoulder · Apr–May · Oct–Nov

Good value. Fewer crowds and lower prices pretty much everywhere. In spring the lakes thaw and the Alps go green quickly, and in autumn the Salzkammergut forests turn gold. We went to Salzburg at the end of March and it wasn't swamped with tour groups at all.

⚠️
Hallstatt Summer (July and August peak): plan around it

The village gets swamped with day-trip coaches and queues for the viewpoints. Go first thing in the morning, or stay the night, to see it properly.

The highlights

Things to do

My quick picks. There's more detail in the city guides further down.

1. Vienna's Imperial Palaces

Between them, Schönbrunn and the Hofburg tell the whole Habsburg story. Give it a full day and don't skip the gardens.

Tip: Get to Schönbrunn when it opens, before the tour groups turn up. See tours on GetYourGuide

2. Salzburg Old Town

Mozart's birthplace, baroque churches and the fortress above the rooftops. It's small enough to walk everywhere, and there's far more than a day trip's worth to see.

Tip: Stay at least one night. The city empties out after 6pm and feels completely different. See tours on GetYourGuide

3. Hallstatt

It's one of the most photographed villages in Europe, and for good reason. Go early, or stay the night, before the day-trip coaches arrive.

Tip: The viewpoint above the village is worth the 20-min climb. See tours on GetYourGuide

4. Vienna's coffee house culture

Café Central, Landtmann and Hawelka are Vienna institutions. Sit for hours with a Melange and a newspaper, the way locals have done since 1876.

Tip: Order the Apfelstrudel. It's on another level here. See tours on GetYourGuide

5. Hohensalzburg Fortress

One of the best-preserved medieval castles in Europe, and the view over Salzburg's rooftops is worth the trip up on its own.

Tip: Take the funicular up and walk back down. See tours on GetYourGuide

6. Sound of Music tour, Salzburg

Cheesy? Yes. Worth it? Also yes. It gets you round a good chunk of the Salzkammergut, and the filming locations are beautiful. (We did the free self-guided walk round the city instead, and loved it.)

Tip: The half-day version covers all the key spots. See tours on GetYourGuide

7. Vienna's museum quarter

The Kunsthistorisches Museum could fill a day on its own, with Vermeer, Bruegel and Raphael all under one roof.

Tip: If you only do two, make it the KHM and the Belvedere (for Klimt's Kiss). See tours on GetYourGuide

8. Skiing the Austrian Alps

Kitzbühel, St Anton and Zell am See are some of the best-known resorts in Europe, and they're easier to get to from Salzburg or Innsbruck than you'd think. The one I've skied is Obergurgl, right at the top of the Ötztal.

Tip: Go between December and March, and book passes and lessons ahead in the busy weeks. See tours on GetYourGuide

Good to know

Things I wish I'd known

🚂
The train is better than flying

Vienna to Salzburg is 2h 20min on ÖBB. That's quicker than flying once you count getting to and from the airports, and the views through the Alps are half the fun.

🏘️
Hallstatt is tiny

About 800 people live there, and it can get 10,000 visitors a day. Go on a weekday, get there before 9am, or stay the night to see what it's really like.

☕
Austrian coffee culture has rules

A Melange isn't quite a latte and a Verlängerter isn't quite an Americano. If you're not sure, ask the waiter and they'll sort you out.

💳
Cash still matters

Smaller restaurants, market stalls and guesthouses out in the Salzkammergut often don't take cards, so carry €50–100 in cash.

🎄
Christmas market season is worth timing for

From late November through December, Vienna and Salzburg have some of the best Christmas markets in Europe, and they're properly atmospheric.

🎟️
The Vienna City Card is worth it

You get unlimited public transport and museum discounts, so if you're in Vienna for more than a day it pays for itself quickly.
