Austria: the country guide

Imperial palaces, Mozart's birthplace, a lake village so perfect it looks painted, and skiing the rest of Europe measures itself against. Here's what it costs, when to go and what to do.

In this guide

  5. Where to go in Austria
  6. Skiing in Austria

Austria is small, but it fits a lot in. Vienna is one of the great European capitals — imperial architecture, world-class museums, coffee houses that have barely changed in a century. Two hours west by train, Salzburg is Mozart, Baroque spires and the Alps at its doorstep. And in between, Hallstatt sits at the edge of a mountain lake so ridiculously picturesque it holds a UNESCO designation and its own unofficial clone in China. You can do all three in a week and feel like you've barely scratched it.

Timing

When to go

Summer · June — September

Best for sightseeing. Long days, warm weather and every lake, trail and terrace fully open. Hallstatt is at peak beauty — and peak crowds. Book accommodation weeks ahead in July and August.

Winter · December — March

Best for atmosphere. Vienna and Salzburg's Christmas markets are worth the trip, not just tourist traps. Skiing opens from December. Hallstatt under snow is beautiful, with fewer tourists too.

Shoulder · Apr–May · Oct–Nov

Good value. Fewer crowds and lower prices across the board. Spring sees the lakes thaw and the Alps green up fast. Autumn turns the Salzkammergut forests gold — one of Europe's best leaf seasons.

⚠️
Hallstatt Summer (Jul — Aug peak): plan around it

The village gets overwhelmed — day-tripper buses, queues for viewpoints, no room to breathe. Go early morning or stay overnight to see it properly.

The highlights

Things to do

Quick picks. The full detail is in the city guides further down.

1. Vienna's Imperial Palaces

Schönbrunn and the Hofburg between them tell the entire Habsburg story — allocate a full day and don't skip the gardens.

Tip: Schönbrunn at opening time before the tour groups arrive. See tours on GetYourGuide

2. Salzburg Old Town

Mozart's birthplace, Baroque churches and the fortress above the rooftops — compact, walkable and more than a day trip's worth.

Tip: Stay at least one night — the city empties and transforms after 6pm. See tours on GetYourGuide

3. Hallstatt

The most photographed village in Europe — deservedly so, but go early or stay overnight before the day-trip buses arrive.

Tip: The viewpoint above the village is worth the 20-min climb. See tours on GetYourGuide

4. Vienna's coffee house culture

Café Central, Landtmann and Hawelka are institutions — sit for hours with a Melange and a newspaper, the way it's been done since 1876.

Tip: Order the Apfelstrudel — it's different here. See tours on GetYourGuide

5. Hohensalzburg Fortress

One of the best-preserved medieval castles in Europe, with a view over Salzburg's rooftops that earns its own trip.

Tip: Take the funicular up, walk the trail back down. See tours on GetYourGuide

6. Sound of Music tour — Salzburg

Cheesy? Yes. Worth it? Also yes — it covers half the Salzkammergut region and the filming locations are beautiful.

Tip: The half-day version covers all the key spots. See tours on GetYourGuide

7. Vienna's museum quarter

The Kunsthistorisches alone could fill a day — Vermeer, Bruegel, Raphael all under one Habsburg roof.

Tip: The KHM and Belvedere (Klimt's Kiss) are the two must-dos. See tours on GetYourGuide

8. Skiing the Austrian Alps

Kitzbühel, St Anton and Zell am See are world-class — and more accessible from Salzburg or Innsbruck than most people realise.

Tip: Dec–March; book ski passes and lessons ahead in peak weeks. See tours on GetYourGuide

Good to know

What most guides don't tell you

🚂
The train is better than flying

Vienna to Salzburg is 2h 20min on ÖBB — faster city-centre to city-centre than flying, and the scenery through the Alps is half the experience.

🏘️
Hallstatt is tiny

About 800 residents, no room for 10,000 daily visitors. Go on a weekday, arrive before 9am, or stay the night and see it as it actually is.

☕
Austrian coffee culture has rules

A Melange is not a latte. A Verlängerter is not an Americano. Ask the waiter — they'll tell you what you actually want.

💳
Cash still matters

Smaller restaurants, market stalls and rural guesthouses in the Salzkammergut often don't take cards. Carry €50–100 in cash.

🎄
Christmas market season is worth timing for

Late November through December, Vienna and Salzburg have some of the best in Europe — not just another tourist trap, atmospheric.

🎟️
The Vienna City Card is worth it

Unlimited public transport plus museum discounts — if you're spending more than a day in Vienna, it pays for itself quickly.
