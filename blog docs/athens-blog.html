Athens
A Dazzling Maze

Athens is a dazzling maze of streets, with archaeological marvels around every corner and beneath every footstep. The Acropolis, designed to be seen from every corner of the city, stands proud above it, a crowning jewel.

Arriving in Athens & Kolonaki

I spent five days in Athens, sharing an Airbnb with family located in the up-scale, undeniably expensive district of Kolonaki. We arrived into Athens airport, located a manageable hour away from the city centre, in the early afternoon, but regrettably faced the longest passport control line I have ever seen.

Tired and sweaty, we stepped off the metro into the centre of Athens, and walked the ten minutes from the nearest metro to our apartment. Kolonaki is stunning, with immaculate pavements and cute boutique shops and restaurants, though we would later find that many of these are…extortionately priced. But the district is peaceful, and conveniently located, making it a good base for a trip to the city.

Located a short walk away are the National Gardens, which we explored first, one of the only spots in the city where you can embrace relative silence and nature. By walking through the gardens, you arrive into the centre of the city, with bustling roads packed with restaurants and shops. It was here we ate almost every night.

💡
Getting from the airport

The metro takes around an hour to the centre and is far cheaper than a taxi. Budget extra time if you're arriving on a busy international route — passport control queues can be brutal.

Day Two

The Acropolis & City Tour

On the second day, we woke early for a guided tour of the Acropolis, including: the Parthenon, Propylaea (the grand entrance), temples, and a stunning Roman-era theatre named Odeon of Herodes Atticus. Our guide was incredible, with an archaeological background that provided so much insight into everything we saw.

The views over the city cannot be matched, with the Acropolis enshrined as the tallest point in Athens ensuring the city always sprawls out beneath your feet. My one and only suggestion for a visit here would be to ensure you come outside of peak tourist season. The Acropolis was busy for us in early April, and would be an absolute nightmare (I imagine) in the peak of summer, with too many people to see the buildings around you and the white stone turning the site into an oven.

If you do go in peak season, promise me you will at least go in the early morning or late afternoon to avoid the sun.

— Athens, April

Leaving the Acropolis, our tour continued into the city, taking us first to the Hellenic Parliament to watch the changing of the guard ceremony, and then to the Olympic stadium, and on to Monastriaki Square. Here, we were left to our own devices to explore this corner of Athens. The markets here are sprawling, and sure to promise an antique bargain. With streets lined with traditional restaurants (and some tourist traps) this is the place to experience a gyros.

⚠️
Choosing where to eat in Monastiraki

In the wise words of our tour guide: don't eat somewhere where they serve multiple Greek staples — it's like expecting a restaurant serving burgers and pizzas to be good at both.

Day Three

A Day at the Coast

On day three, we decided we needed a break from the hustle and bustle of the city. Luckily, you can get to the coast easily from the centre of Athens, by bus. It takes approximately forty minutes — Athens is a gigantic city — and though the bus can be very busy, the network is simple to navigate.

Arriving in Glyfada, we spent much of the day lounging on perfect beaches and walking along the coast. Though the area doesn't have too much to offer in terms of facilities or shops, we managed to secure ourselves coffees and lunch before returning to Athens for dinner.

💡
Getting to Glyfada

The bus from central Athens takes around forty minutes and is considerably cheaper than a taxi. The network is straightforward — just allow extra time as it can get busy.

Day Four

Island Hopping by Pirate Ship

Our last full day was by far my favourite. It was a long day, and not one I would recommend for those who get seasick, but an island-hopping boat tour is top of my list for activities from Athens. Aboard an old-fashioned pirate ship, we set sail for Agistri, with its stunning turquoise waters and a tranquil silence that you just don't find in Athens. We saw the island's beautiful blue-topped Church, and sipped a coffee at a waterfront café and bar.

Back on the boat, we were served the best lunch I had in Greece, and given the chance to jump off the boat. An opportunity that I chose not to take…it was April…the water was freezing. Then, we were shown an uninhabited island from the boat, before disembarking at Aegina, or pistachio island!

Here, we paid to visit an archaeological site, where the ruins weren't worth it for me of their own accord, but the view they offered of the coast was absolutely worth every penny. We, of course, bought some pistachios in various interesting flavours, and a pistachio Mr Whippy ice cream, before heading back to the boat for our return to land. On the journey back, we were plied with free wine and beer which, coupled with the stunning ocean views, was the perfect way to end the day.

The short version

The island-hopping boat tour is the single best thing you can do from Athens. A full day at sea, two islands, a brilliant lunch, and free drinks on the way home. Book in advance — spots go fast.

Last Day

The National Archaeological Museum

On our last day, we hastily stored our luggage near the bus stop to the airport, and boarded a bus taking us to the National Archaeological Museum. The building has a stunningly huge collection of just about everything you might want to learn about, and you can honestly get lost walking through its halls.

📌
Museum entry costs

Unfortunately, museum entry is quite expensive, especially if you're a non-EU citizen — but it's absolutely worth it. Factor this into your budget before you go.

Getting There & Getting Around

Athens airport is a manageable hour from the city centre. On our last day, the bus back to the airport took about an hour and was considerably cheaper than taking the metro — worth knowing for departure day.

AirportAthens International (ATH) — approximately one hour from the city centre by metro or bus.
MetroSimple to navigate and the easiest way in from the airport. Buy a day pass if you're planning to use it frequently.
Bus to coastAround forty minutes to Glyfada. Cheap and straightforward, though can get busy.
Passport controlNon-EU queues at ATH can be extremely long on busy arrival days. Build in extra time.
Card / cashCards are widely accepted in central Athens, though smaller restaurants and market stalls may prefer cash.

Athens is a city that earns its reputation. Whether you're standing on the Acropolis at sunrise, wandering the markets of Monastiraki, or eating the best lunch of your life on a boat off the coast of Agistri, it delivers at every turn. Go outside of peak summer if you can, hire a guide for the Acropolis, and don't skip the island-hopping tour. You will not regret it.

— Blake Wirtz, Athens · April 2026
