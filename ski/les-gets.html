Les Gets: the resort I keep going back to

I have been to Les Gets about seven times, mostly with family and sometimes with family friends. It is the resort I know best, and the one I would send most people to first.

Les Gets is a proper village rather than a purpose-built resort, and it sits in the Portes du Soleil, a huge linked area that runs across France and Switzerland. You can ski from Les Gets into Morzine, and from there on to Avoriaz and the Swiss resorts if your legs are up to it.

The trips

How I did it

Most of my trips were about five days of skiing. Honestly, three days is enough to cover the Les Gets side properly, so if you are short on time or money it works well as a long weekend. Five days lets you get into Morzine and further.

It is a really good family resort. The ski schools here are very good, which is a big part of why we kept coming back with younger skiers in the group.

Getting there

Getting there

Flying · Geneva

Every time I flew it was into Geneva and then a transfer up to the resort, which takes roughly an hour and a quarter in normal traffic. I search flights on Kiwi.com, and for the airport run you can compare shared and private transfers on GetTransfer. Saturday transfers in peak weeks are slower, so give yourself time.

Driving · from England

I have also driven from England. It is a long day, but you can take as much kit as you like and it works out well split between a full car. Through France it is mostly motorway with tolls, and the last stretch climbs up from Cluses.

🚆
The lower-carbon way: train to Cluses

I have not done this one myself yet, but it is the greener option. Get the Eurostar to Paris, a TGV towards the Alps, and change for Cluses, the nearest station. Buses run from Cluses up to Les Gets, about half an hour. It is a full day of travel, but no flight and no car.

Where I stayed

Where I stayed

We have stayed in a few different places over the years. The one that stands out is Hôtel Le Christiania, right in the village. It is an easy base for the lifts and the village, and it has its own restaurant and bar, which is handy at the end of a long day.

Staying in the village means you can walk to the main lifts and the shops, and you ski back down to it at the end of the day. That run home is one of my favourite parts of the resort.

The skiing

My favourite runs

The Yeti · Chavannes, black

The Yeti is the one I always go back to. It is the black off the Rosta chairlift on the Chavannes side, with bumps just below the lift, so everyone on the chair gets to watch you. Great fun if you are a confident skier.

The Indian run · Chavannes

The Indian-themed run with the teepees is a favourite, and not only for kids. It has bumps and turns a bit like a boardercross, and it is the place you will hear the most laughing on the mountain.

The Bordercross and the freeride area

The boardercross is brilliant if you like a bit of speed and banked turns, and the freeride area is a good, safer way to try skiing off the groomed runs. Both are worth a few goes.

The run down to the village

Ending the day by skiing all the way back into the village never gets old. It is an easy way to finish, and you come out right by the bars.

Morzine

Over to Morzine

Morzine is linked to Les Gets, so you can ski across and back in a day on the same pass. Most of my Morzine skiing was done that way. I stayed in Morzine once as well, but I honestly can not remember much about that trip, so I will not pretend I can give you a full guide to it.

What I can say is that Morzine is a bigger town than Les Gets, with more going on in the evenings. It is also the gateway to Avoriaz higher up. If you want quieter evenings and a simpler family base, Les Gets is my pick.

For Avoriaz and the Swiss side, see my Champéry and Avoriaz guide.

Food and après

Food and après

I genuinely can not remember the names of the places we ate across seven trips, so these are not my own recommendations. They are well-known spots I would try next time.

On the mountainLe Yéti on Mont Chéry for a hot chocolate stop. La Grande Ourse at 1,750m for a proper sit-down lunch.
In the villageLes Copeaux for tartiflette and raclette. La Fruitière des Perrières for fondue. Primo for pizza.
Plant-basedLa Piste Noire at Hotel Marmotte has a mostly plant-based menu.
AprèsL'AprèSki Bar and Le Barbylone are just off the slopes. The Christiania bar does cocktails.
Tips

What I'd tell a friend

❄️
Go when the snow is more predictable

Les Gets is not a high resort, so snow can be hit and miss early and late in the season. Go in the heart of the winter, roughly January to early March, when the snow is more reliable.

📅
Avoid school holidays

The slopes, the lift queues and the prices all get worse in UK and French school holidays. If you can go outside them, do.

🎿
Book ski school early

The ski schools are very good, which also means they fill up. If you have beginners or kids in your group, book lessons before you go.
