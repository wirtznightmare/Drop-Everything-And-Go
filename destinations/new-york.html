USA

Eight million stories, one island, and an energy unlike anywhere else on the planet.

Americas · USA

Why visit New York City?

New York is one of those cities that works as a myth before you arrive — and then somehow exceeds the myth. The energy is real. The food (every cuisine on earth, at every price point) is real. The architecture is real. The neighbourhoods, each with their own culture, are real. It rewards repeat visits and deep exploration far more than a checklist approach. First-timers: don't try to see everything. Pick a few neighbourhoods and actually live in them for a few days.

Know Before You Go

Where to Base Yourself in New York

New York isn't one-size-fits-all. Each area has a completely different character — here's how to choose.

🗽

Manhattan (Midtown)

Best for: First-time visitors, those wanting the iconic New York experience

The classic New York of Times Square, Rockefeller Center, Fifth Avenue and Central Park. The Empire State Building and One World Observatory are the essential viewpoints. The High Line (an elevated park on a former rail line) is genuinely excellent and connects the Meatpacking District to Hudson Yards. Grand Central Terminal is one of the world's great buildings — go during rush hour to watch the choreography.

🌉

Lower Manhattan & Brooklyn Bridge

Best for: History lovers, photographers, those wanting free New York experiences

Wall Street, the 9/11 Memorial (book in advance), the Oculus transit hub, and the Staten Island Ferry (free, best views of the Statue of Liberty without paying for the tour). Walk the Brooklyn Bridge — the view of lower Manhattan from the bridge's midpoint is the best free view in the city. DUMBO (Down Under the Manhattan Bridge Overpass) has the famous cobblestone-street-through-bridge-arch photograph.

🎨

Brooklyn

Best for: Those wanting off-the-tourist-trail New York, food lovers, art enthusiasts, nightlife

New York's most interesting borough — Williamsburg (music venues, vintage markets, excellent food), Bushwick (the largest outdoor street art gallery in the world), Park Slope (family-friendly brownstones and Prospect Park), and Red Hook (repurposed warehouses, the best tacos in the city at Red Hook ballfields). Brooklyn is where New York actually lives.

🎷

Harlem & Upper Manhattan

Best for: Culture lovers, jazz enthusiasts, those wanting New York's cultural history

The spiritual home of African-American culture in New York — jazz was born here, the Harlem Renaissance happened here, and the neighbourhood is experiencing a renaissance of its own. The Apollo Theater still hosts Amateur Night. Sylvia's Restaurant on Lenox Avenue is the legendary soul food institution. The Cloisters museum (medieval art in a medieval building reconstructed on a Hudson River hilltop) is extraordinary.

🌍

Queens & The Bronx

Best for: Food lovers, those wanting authentic immigrant New York, the genuinely curious traveller

The most ethnically diverse borough on earth (Queens) and the birthplace of hip-hop (the Bronx). Jackson Heights in Queens has the best Indian and Bangladeshi food outside South Asia. Flushing is the finest Chinatown in America. The New York Botanical Garden in the Bronx is world-class. Arthur Avenue in the Bronx is the real Italian-American neighbourhood that Little Italy in Manhattan pretends to be.
