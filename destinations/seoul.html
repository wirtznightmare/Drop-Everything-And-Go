// East Asia · South Korea

Seoul

Ancient palaces, futuristic towers, Korean BBQ and clubs that run until 8am.

💰 £40–£70/day 🗓️ Best: Apr–Jun & Sep–Nov 🛂 No visa (up to 90 days) 💬 Korean (English in tourist areas)

K-culture, and then some

Seoul moves at extraordinary speed — from Joseon Dynasty palaces and Buddhist temples to glass-and-steel towers and neon-lit entertainment districts — and it does everything with a precision and energy that is hard to match. The food alone deserves a dedicated trip.

Korean BBQ, bibimbap, tteokbokki, late-night pojangmacha street food tents — Seoul's food culture is world-class and genuinely affordable. Add the DMZ just 60km north, hiking above the city and the extraordinary K-beauty scene, and Seoul is easily worth a full week.

Daily Budget
£40–70. Street food from £2, restaurants £5–12. Guesthouses from £15/night.

Apr–Jun (cherry blossom, perfect weather) and Sep–Nov (golden autumn).

Cards
T-Money card for all transport and convenience stores.

Kakao T for taxis — metered and reliable. Download before arriving.
// Top Experiences

What to actually do

01
Gyeongbokgung & Guard Ceremony
The grandest Joseon palace — the 10am and 2pm changing of the royal guard is spectacular. Rent a hanbok (traditional dress) for free entry and excellent photographs.
02
Bukchon Hanok Village
600-year-old wooden Korean houses on the hillside between two palaces — the most atmospheric district in Seoul, best before 9am.
03
Korean BBQ in Mapo District
The neighbourhood around Mapo and Sinchon has Seoul's best BBQ streets. Charcoal-grilled samgyeopsal (pork belly) with soju — a complete cultural experience.
04
DMZ & Joint Security Area Tour
The most militarised border in the world — 60km north of Seoul. The JSA tour requires booking and is genuinely chilling as geopolitical theatre.
05
Hongdae Street Food & Nightlife
The university neighbourhood is Seoul's liveliest — street performers, live music, fried chicken tents and clubs that run past dawn.
06
Namsan Tower at Night
The Seoul Tower on Namsan mountain with the city spread below at night — take the cable car and walk the circular path for 360° city views.
// Areas & Neighbourhoods

Where to base yourself

Jongno & Insadong
History, culture, traditional food
The historic heart — Gyeongbokgung Palace, Bukchon hanok village and Insadong's antique shops and traditional teahouses.
Itaewon & Yongsan
International food, nightlife
The international district — diverse food, the War Memorial museum and some of the best cocktail bars in the city.
Hongdae & Gangnam
Nightlife, K-pop, shopping
The contrast: Hongdae is the creative, youthful area. Gangnam is the ultra-modern, ultra-wealthy district — yes, that song is about this place.

// When to Go

Season guide

Apr–Jun: Cherry blossom in April, warm spring weather — the best time to visit. Sep–Nov: Autumn foliage and comfortable temperatures — October is particularly good. Dec–Feb: Very cold and dry — ski resorts above the city are excellent. Jul–Aug: Hot and very humid. The monsoon (changma) brings heavy rain in July.

// Need to Know

Before you go

🚇

Get a T-Money card at the airport — works on all metro, buses and at convenience stores. Extremely cheap transport system.

🍗

Tipping is not customary in Korea. Payment is often at a counter or machine, not to the server.

🗣️

Learn a few Korean phrases — even kamsahamnida (thank you) is genuinely appreciated.

💄

Korean skincare products are world-class and cheaper than at home. Olive Young stores and Myeongdong street are best.

🚕

Kakao T is the reliable taxi app — download before arrival. Regular cabs are metered and cheap.
