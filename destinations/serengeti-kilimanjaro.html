Serengeti & Kilimanjaro
Tanzania

The Great Migration, the Ngorongoro Crater and the roof of Africa at 5,895m.

Africa · Tanzania

Why visit Tanzania?

The Great Migration — 1.5 million wildebeest in continuous circular movement between Tanzania and Kenya, with the Mara River crossings between July and October — is the most extraordinary wildlife event on earth. The Ngorongoro Crater below adds a complete ecosystem in a volcanic caldera where every East African large mammal exists in one enclosed valley.

Kilimanjaro stands 5,895m above the surrounding plain — Africa's highest peak, accessible without technical climbing on the 7-day Machame Route. The combination of safari and summit in one Tanzania trip is uniquely compelling.

Who It's Perfect For
🦁

Wildlife Enthusiasts

The Serengeti Migration is the world's greatest wildlife event. Ngorongoro Crater is the world's best single-day safari. Tarangire has extraordinary elephant concentrations.

🏔️

Summit Seekers

Kilimanjaro — Africa's highest point at 5,895m, accessible without technical climbing. The Machame Route in 7 days has the best acclimatisation.

🏖️

Beach & Safari Combiners

The 35-minute flight to Zanzibar makes the classic East Africa combination — bush then beach — uniquely achievable in one trip.

The Highlights

Things to Do & See

01
Serengeti Great Migration safari — the Mara River crossings July–October are the most dramatic wildlife scene on earth
02
Ngorongoro Crater full day drive — every East African large mammal including the rare black rhino
03
Kilimanjaro summit via Machame Route — 7-day ascent to Uhuru Peak at 5,895m
04
Zanzibar extension — Stone Town history and the north coast (Nungwi) beaches after the safari
05
Tarangire dry season safari — thousands of elephants at the river, baobab trees and almost no tourists
06
Selous/Nyerere walking safari — the largest protected area in Africa with walking safaris permitted
07
Maasai village cultural visit — the traditional pastoral communities who live alongside the wildlife
08
Aerial game viewing by light aircraft — the Serengeti from above reveals the scale of the Migration
