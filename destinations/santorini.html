Sunsets that stop time, clifftop villages, and the most photographed views on earth.

Europe · Greece

Why visit Santorini?

Santorini is the kind of place that doesn't look real until you're standing in it. Perched on the rim of a volcanic caldera, its whitewashed villages tumble dramatically towards the deep Aegean. It's romantic, spectacular, and endlessly photogenic — but also genuinely magical if you know where to look beyond the crowds.

Where to Stay

Base Yourself in Santorini

Santorini isn't one-size-fits-all. Each village has a completely different character — here's how to choose.

🌅

Oia

Best for: Couples, photographers, luxury seekers

The most photographed village on earth, perched at the northern tip of the island. Whitewashed houses, blue-domed churches and clifftop terraces with caldera views that stop time. Come for sunset — but arrive two hours early. Beyond the main drag, narrow paths lead to quieter viewpoints and the old port of Ammoudi below, where you eat the freshest seafood in Greece right on the waterfront.

🏙️

Fira

Best for: Budget travellers, solo travellers, first-timers

The island's capital is livelier, louder and more affordable than Oia. It's where most ferry passengers arrive, where the nightlife concentrates, and where you'll find the Museum of Prehistoric Thera (extraordinary artefacts from the Akrotiri excavations). The cable car down to the old port is worth the short queue. Fira is the practical base for exploring the whole island — central, well-connected and honest about what it is.

👁️

Imerovigli

Best for: Luxury travellers, walkers, those wanting fewer tourists

Sitting on the highest point of the caldera rim between Fira and Oia, Imerovigli has some of the most dramatic views on the island with far fewer visitors. The Skaros Rock — a ruined medieval fortress — juts out over the water on a narrow path that requires some nerve. This is where the smartest luxury hotels quietly locate themselves, and where you can walk to both Oia and Fira on the clifftop path.

🏰

Pyrgos

Best for: Authentic experience seekers, those escaping the tourist trail

Inland and away from the caldera crowds, Pyrgos is the island's highest village and feels genuinely untouched. Venetian castle ruins, Byzantine churches and local tavernas that haven't changed their menus in decades. The best sunset on the island that nobody tells you about — and you won't be sharing it with a thousand others. A 15-minute drive from Fira and a completely different world.

🏖️

Perissa & Perivolos

Best for: Beach lovers, younger travellers, those on a budget

The south coast beach towns offer a completely different Santorini — black volcanic sand beaches, beach bars, water sports and a genuinely social scene. Perissa has a backpacker-friendly strip with affordable tavernas and hostels. It's connected to Kamari on the other side of Mesa Vouno mountain by a walking path around the base — one of the island's best short hikes.

Insider Knowledge

5 Things to Know About Santorini

  • →

    The famous Oia sunset is more spectacular from Imerovigli — same view, a fraction of the crowd

  • →

    Rent an ATV for a day (£20–30) — it's the best way to reach the quieter south coast beaches that buses don't serve

  • →

    Santo Wines and Venetsanos winery both have caldera-view tasting terraces — book the sunset slot in advance online

  • →

    The ancient ruins of Akrotiri (the 'Greek Pompeii') are as impressive as anything on the island and consistently under-visited

  • →
