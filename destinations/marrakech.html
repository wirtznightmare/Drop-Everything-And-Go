// Middle East & North Africa

Marrakech & Morocco

Souks, riads, the Sahara and a sensory intensity unlike anywhere else.

💰 £25–£55/day 🗓️ Best: Mar–May & Sep–Nov 🛂 No visa required (up to 90 days) 💬 Arabic, French, some English

The labyrinth and the desert

Marrakech hits you immediately. The calls to prayer, the smell of the spice market, the hammered copper, the labyrinthine medina that refuses to make sense — it is one of the most disorienting and exhilarating places on earth. It takes a day to find your bearings, and then you never want to leave.

Morocco is far more than Marrakech. Fes has the world's largest medieval medina and the centuries-old tanneries. The Sahara around Merzouga has 150m dunes and Berber camps for nights under the stars. Chefchaouen is the extraordinary blue-washed hill town in the Rif mountains. Morocco rewards a circuit over a single city.

// Quick Facts

💰
Daily Budget
£25–55. Riad guesthouses typically include breakfast — often the best meal in Morocco.
🏡
Riads
Traditional courtyard guesthouses are the best accommodation in Morocco — atmospheric and competitively priced.
🚢
Getting There
Easy from UK — regular flights to Marrakech and Casablanca. Tangier accessible by ferry from Spain.
💬
French
More useful than English in Morocco — even basic French goes a long way, especially outside tourist areas.
🧭
Navigation
Medinas are designed to confuse outsiders. A guide for the first half-day prevents hours of being lost.
// Top Experiences

What to actually do

01
Djemaa el-Fna at Sunset
The UNESCO main square transforms at dusk — storytellers, snake charmers, rows of food stalls and musicians creating the most atmospheric public square in Africa.
02
Marrakech Souks with a Guide
The 14 specialist souks (spice, leather, carpet, metal, fabric) in the lanes north of the square — a morning with a guide prevents both getting lost and the most aggressive selling.
03
Fes Medina & the Chouara Tanneries
The world's largest living medieval city — 9,000 alleyways and the extraordinary tanneries where leather has been dyed by hand in the same circular stone vats for 1,000 years.
04
Sahara Desert Overnight Camp
Camel trek at sunset into Erg Chebbi's 150m dunes, sleep in a Berber camp under the Milky Way and wake for sunrise from the sand ridge.
05
Chefchaouen Blue City
The Rif mountain town painted entirely in shades of blue — every alley is a photograph. Best in early morning before the day trippers arrive from the coast.
06
Atlas Mountain Berber Trek
Day hike or multi-day trek from Imlil (1 hour from Marrakech) through Berber villages and argan groves to views of Toubkal — North Africa's highest peak.
// Areas & Neighbourhoods

Where to base yourself

Marrakech — Medina
Souks, Djemaa el-Fna, riads
Stay inside the medina walls in a riad for the full experience. The Djemaa el-Fna is the heart — the souks radiate north from it.
Fes — Medina
Most authentic, tanneries, crafts
The most authentic imperial city — the medina is a genuine working medieval city, not a tourist attraction. Stay near Bab Bou Jeloud.
Merzouga — Sahara
Desert camping, stargazing
The small town at the base of Erg Chebbi dunes — 3-day 4WD tours cover the flat, the red lagoons and the geysers on the Algerian border.

// When to Go

Season guide

Mar–May: The best time — mild temperatures, spring flowers in the Atlas and Sahara. Sep–Oct: Warm and very pleasant, harvest season in the south. Nov–Feb: Cold in Fes, Chefchaouen and the Atlas. Marrakech comfortable (15–18°C). Jun–Aug: Extremely hot (40°C+) across the south. The coast is significantly cooler.

// Need to Know

Before you go

🗣️

A firm 'la shukran' (no thank you in Arabic) or 'non merci' works for persistent touts. Never follow anyone offering to show you around without agreeing a price first.

👔

Dress modestly in medinas — covered shoulders and knees for both men and women. Genuinely appreciated and prevents hassle.

💰

Bargaining in souks is expected — start at 40% of the asking price and meet in the middle. Never enter a negotiation you're not prepared to complete.

🏡

Riads include breakfast — often the best food you'll eat in Morocco. Fresh msemen (flatbread), argan oil, olives and orange blossom coffee.

🚌

The train from Casablanca to Fes and Marrakech is excellent. CTM buses for routes not covered by rail.
