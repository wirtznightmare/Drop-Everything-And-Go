// Northern Europe · UK

London

Endlessly deep, endlessly walkable — one of the world's truly great cities.

💰 £80–£140/day 🗓️ Best: May–Sep & Dec 🛂 eTA required for most non-UK/Irish nationals (check current requirements) 💬 English

World-class museums, free entry

London is one of the few cities that gets better the more you know it. The first visit is the obvious things — and they are genuinely extraordinary. The British Museum, the view from Parliament Hill, the Thames at low tide. The second visit is the neighbourhoods: Brixton Market, the Canal in Hackney on a Sunday, the Barbican estate, the rooftop allotments in Peckham.

The great thing about London is that most of its best attractions are free. The British Museum, National Gallery, V&A, Natural History Museum, Tate Modern — all free. The city is expensive in other ways but the cultural infrastructure is the most generous in the world.

// Top Experiences

What to actually do

01
British Museum — Know What to See
The world's greatest collection of human history — the Rosetta Stone, Elgin Marbles, Sutton Hoo helmet. Free entry. Focus on Rooms 1–4 and the Egyptian galleries.
02
Tate Modern & the South Bank
The world's most visited modern art museum in a converted power station — walk the full South Bank from London Bridge to the Globe Theatre with a coffee.
03
Borough Market on a Thursday or Saturday
The oldest food market in London (1,000+ years) — Neal's Yard cheese, Monmouth coffee, Spanish ham and the best lunch for £8 in any market hall in the UK.
04
Hackney & East London on a Sunday
Brick Lane market, Broadway Market, Ridley Road and the Regent's Canal towpath walk — the most interesting 4 hours in London if you want to understand the city.
05
Parliament Hill Panoramic View
The hill in Hampstead Heath with the most complete London skyline — free, no queues, no crowds. Best at golden hour looking south over the city.
06
National Gallery — A Specific Strategy
Free entry, world-class — Sainsbury Wing (van Eyck, Botticelli, Raphael) and Room 34 (Turner, Constable). Don't attempt everything. Two rooms done properly.
// Areas & Neighbourhoods

Where to base yourself

South Bank & Southwark
Art, food market, river walk
Tate Modern, Borough Market, the Globe, the Golden Hinde and the best riverside walking in the city. Completely free for an entire day.
Shoreditch & Hackney
Street art, food, creative scene
East London's creative core — the best restaurants, coffee shops and street art. Columbia Road Flower Market on Sunday morning is unmissable.
Notting Hill & Portobello Road
Saturday market, vintage, neighbourhood
Portobello Road antique market on Saturday, the Electric Cinema and the best independent boutiques in London.

// When to Go

Season guide

May–Sep: Best weather and outdoor events. June–August is peak but London handles crowds across its 33 boroughs. Apr: Easter is excellent — fewer tourists and the parks are beautiful. Dec: Christmas lights and markets are spectacular. Nov–Feb: Grey and cold but cheapest accommodation.

// Need to Know

Before you go

🚇

Always Oyster or contactless — never paper tickets. The daily cap prevents overspending regardless of how many journeys.

🍽️

Eat in the neighbourhoods not the tourist areas — the restaurant density in Brixton, Peckham, Hackney and Shoreditch is world-class and much better value.

🖼️

London's free museums are extraordinary. Never pay for what you can see for free — budget the money saved for Borough Market or a neighbourhood dinner instead.

🚶

London is best explored on foot — the city reveals itself at walking pace in a way no map or tube journey can replicate.

📱

The Elizabeth Line has transformed east-west transport — faster and more comfortable than the Tube for longer cross-city journeys.
