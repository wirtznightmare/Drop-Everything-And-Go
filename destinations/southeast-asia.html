// Southeast Asia

Southeast
Asia

The classic backpacker circuit — from Thai islands to Vietnamese street food, Balinese temples to Cambodian ruins.

Southeast Asia is where most people fall in love with travelling. The combination of extraordinary food, ancient history, ridiculous natural beauty and genuine affordability is hard to beat anywhere in the world. You can do it on £15/day in Cambodia or splash out in a Bali villa for a fraction of European prices — the range is extraordinary. The classic overland route runs Bangkok → Chiang Mai → Laos → Vietnam → Cambodia → (fly to) Bali, or in reverse. Most people do 4–8 weeks and wish they had longer.

// When to Go

Season guide

Nov–Feb is the dry season for most of mainland Southeast Asia (Thailand, Vietnam north, Cambodia) and generally the best time to visit. March–May is hot and dry before the monsoon hits. June–October is monsoon season on the mainland — heavy rain, especially July–August — but the islands on the east coast of Thailand (Koh Samui, Koh Tao) have their own dry season in this window. Bali has its dry season May–September. There is genuinely no bad time to visit Southeast Asia if you match your route to the weather.

// Need to Know

Before you go

ℹ️

Visa on arrival available for most Western passports in Thailand, Indonesia, Cambodia and Vietnam. Always check current requirements before travel.

ℹ️

The classic backpacker route is roughly 2–3 months. You can do a highlights circuit in 4–6 weeks but you'll wish you had more time.

ℹ️

Scams are common at transport hubs — book buses and trains through guesthouses or reputable apps (12Go Asia), not from street touts.

ℹ️

Travel insurance is non-negotiable in Southeast Asia. Medical care is good in Bangkok and Ho Chi Minh City but expensive for foreigners.

ℹ️

The food from street stalls is almost always safer than tourist restaurants — higher turnover means fresher ingredients.

ℹ️

Learn to say 'thank you' in each country's language. It goes further than you think.
