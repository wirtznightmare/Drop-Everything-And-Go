Peru

Machu Picchu, the Sacred Valley, and Lima — one of the world's great food capitals.

Americas · South America

Why visit Peru?

Peru contains multitudes — one of the Seven Wonders of the World, South America's most acclaimed food scene, the world's highest navigable lake, Amazon rainforest, Pacific coastline, and a cultural depth that spans 5,000 years of civilisation. It's also one of the most trekking-obsessed destinations on earth: the Inca Trail is the most famous but not the most beautiful. Give Peru at least two weeks — ideally three.

Know Before You Go

Where to Base Yourself in Peru

Peru isn't one-size-fits-all. Each area has a completely different character — here's how to choose.

🏛️

Cusco

Best for: Everyone visiting Machu Picchu — it's the essential base

The ancient Inca capital sits at 3,400 metres — give yourself 48 hours to acclimatise before doing anything strenuous. The Plaza de Armas is surrounded by Spanish colonial architecture built on top of Inca stone walls (you can see the join). The San Pedro Market is the city's real kitchen. The nightlife on Calle del Medio is surprisingly good. Cusco is the base for all Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu trips.

🏔️

Machu Picchu

Best for: Everyone — it's as good as the photographs suggest

The most spectacular archaeological site in the Americas — a 15th-century Inca city built on a ridge between two mountain peaks, hidden from the Spanish and forgotten for 400 years. Take the bus from Aguas Calientes (the town below) or hike the Sun Gate trail. The site opens at 6am — the first two hours are the best, before the tour groups arrive. Sun Gate at dawn gives the classic panoramic view.

🌄

Sacred Valley

Best for: History lovers, those wanting the context around Machu Picchu, photographers

The valley between Cusco and Machu Picchu is not just a transit route — it's one of the most beautiful landscapes in South America. The Inca salt pans of Maras (hundreds of terraced pools that have been harvesting salt since Inca times), the circular terraces of Moray (possibly an agricultural research station), and the enormous fortress of Ollantaytambo (still inhabited on its ancient Inca street plan) are all essential.

🍽️

Lima

Best for: Food lovers, city enthusiasts, those arriving or departing internationally

South America's food capital — Central (consistently in the world's top 10 restaurants), Maido, Astrid & Gastón, and a street food scene built around ceviche, tiradito and causa. The Miraflores clifftop district has excellent walkways above the Pacific. Barranco is the artists' quarter — a neighbourhood of painted colonial mansions, craft beer bars and the best independent galleries in the city.

⛵

Lake Titicaca

Best for: Cultural travellers, those interested in indigenous communities, photographers

The highest navigable lake in the world, shared between Peru and Bolivia. The Uros floating reed islands are extraordinary — man-made islands built entirely from totora reeds, where Uros communities have lived since pre-Inca times. The island of Taquile has a textile tradition that's a UNESCO Cultural Heritage — the men here knit, not the women, and the hats encode information about the wearer's marital status.

🌿

Amazon (Iquitos & Puerto Maldonado)

Best for: Nature lovers, wildlife enthusiasts, adventurous travellers

Two very different Amazons. Iquitos (reached only by plane or boat) is the world's largest city with no road access — a genuinely isolated jungle city. Puerto Maldonado (southeast, near Bolivia) is more accessible and has the Tambopata National Reserve with excellent lodge-based wildlife watching. Macaws, giant river otters, tapir and over 600 bird species.

Insider Knowledge

5 Things to Know Before You Go to Peru

  • →

    Altitude sickness hits many visitors in Cusco — rest for the first 24 hours, drink coca tea, avoid alcohol, and book accommodation with oxygen available

  • →

    The Inca Trail must be booked 6+ months in advance (only 500 people per day). The Salkantay Trek is wilder, longer and available without a permit

  • →

    Ceviche should be eaten at lunch, not dinner — the best cevicherías in Lima close by 4pm

  • →

    Book the Huayna Picchu mountain hike (above Machu Picchu) when you book the site entry — only 400 spaces per day

  • →

    The scenic train from Cusco to Puno along Lake Titicaca (Andean Explorer) is one of South America's great rail journeys
