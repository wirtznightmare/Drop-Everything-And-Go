Vietnam

Halong Bay's limestone karsts, Hoi An's lantern-lit streets, and the world's best banh mi.

Asia · Southeast Asia

Why visit Vietnam?

Vietnam is one of the great travel countries — 1,600km of coastline, a cuisine that varies dramatically between north and south, jaw-dropping natural scenery, and a culture that's simultaneously ancient and electric with new energy. It rewards slow travel. The mistake is rushing it. Spend three weeks minimum if you want to understand more than the surface.

Where to Stay

Base Yourself in Vietnam

Vietnam isn't one-size-fits-all. Each area has a completely different character — here's how to choose.

🏮

Hanoi

Best for: History lovers, food enthusiasts, those wanting to understand Vietnam's complexity

The north's capital is chaotic, historic and endlessly fascinating. The Old Quarter's 36 streets (each traditionally specialising in a different trade) remain largely intact. The Hoan Kiem Lake and Ngoc Son Temple in its centre is where the city breathes each morning. Vietnamese coffee culture started here — ca phe trung (egg coffee) is a Hanoi invention that shouldn't work but absolutely does.

⛵

Ha Long Bay

Best for: Nature lovers, adventure travellers, anyone who loves being on the water

2,000 limestone karsts rising from emerald water — one of the world's great natural landscapes. Do it properly: book a 2-night cruise rather than a day trip. The further north you go (Lan Ha Bay, Bai Tu Long) the fewer boats you share the view with. Kayaking through hidden lagoons and swimming in water so clear you can see the bottom 10 metres down.

🏮

Hội An

Best for: Couples, culture lovers, food enthusiasts, photographers

A perfectly preserved ancient trading port — the most beautiful town in Southeast Asia. Lantern-lit streets at night, tailors who can make a suit in 24 hours, the best cao lau noodles in Vietnam, and beaches (An Bang, Cua Dai) 4km away by bicycle. It's touristy but handles it gracefully. Get up at 6am and walk the old town before the day trips arrive.

👑

Hue

Best for: History lovers, food enthusiasts, those wanting fewer tourists

The former imperial capital sits on the Perfume River and contains Vietnam's most significant historical monuments — the Imperial Citadel, the royal tombs scattered through the surrounding hills, and pagodas that date back centuries. The food here is distinct from the rest of Vietnam: Hue was the royal kitchen and the cuisine reflects it. Bun bo Hue (spicy beef noodle soup) is the dish.

🏙️

Ho Chi Minh City

Best for: History, nightlife, street food, those flying in or out of the south

The south's powerhouse — faster, louder and more modern than Hanoi. The War Remnants Museum is essential, uncomfortable viewing. The Cu Chi tunnels (book a tour) provide context that no book can replicate. But HCMC is also a city of extraordinary street food, rooftop bars, and the best café culture in Vietnam. District 1 is tourist-central; District 3 is where the city actually lives.

🏔️

Sapa

Best for: Trekkers, nature lovers, those seeking authentic cultural experiences

Mountain town in the far north, close to the Chinese border. The terraced rice fields surrounding it (Muong Hoa valley, Y Linh Ho village) are among the most beautiful landscapes in Asia. Stay in a homestay with a local Hmong or Dao family rather than a town hotel — the experience is incomparable. Best visited March–May or September–November; the summer monsoon obscures the mountain views.

Insider Knowledge

5 Things to Know About Vietnam

  • →

    The Reunification Express train from Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City stops at all the major cities — buy a soft sleeper and use it as transport and accommodation

  • →

    Grab (Southeast Asia's Uber) is reliable, cheap and takes the stress out of motorbike taxis

  • →

    Pho is a northern dish — the best pho bo (beef noodle soup) in the country is in Hanoi, not HCMC

  • →

    Always check whether your visa requires application in advance — the e-visa system covers most nationalities but check before you fly

  • →

    Budget accommodation in Hoi An is excellent — you can stay well for £15–25/night in the old town
