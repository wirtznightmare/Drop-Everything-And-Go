// Western Europe · Spain

Barcelona

Gaudí's impossible buildings, a medieval old town and the Mediterranean at the end of the street.

💰 £55–£95/day 🗓️ Best: May–Jun & Sep–Oct 🛂 No visa (Schengen, 90 days) 💬 Catalan and Spanish (English widely spoken)

Architecture, tapas and sea

Barcelona does something no other city quite manages — world-class architecture, a genuine beach, Michelin-starred restaurants, a medieval old town and one of Europe's great nightlife scenes all within a walkable city. The Sagrada Família is unlike any building you've ever seen. La Boqueria market on a Tuesday morning is one of the great food experiences in Europe.

It is busy. Very busy in summer. But book ahead, stay in the Eixample or Gràcia rather than Las Ramblas, get to Sagrada Família at first entry and Barcelona becomes magnificent.

// Top Experiences

What to actually do

01
Sagrada Família at First Entry — 9am
Gaudí's perpetually unfinished basilica — the interior with stained glass flooding the forest of stone columns is genuinely overwhelming. Book the tower lift for the full experience.
02
Park Güell Mosaic Terrace
The famous terrace and gingerbread gatehouse — book the monumental zone ticket in advance. Then walk the free park area and the Eixample for Gaudí's other buildings.
03
Gothic Quarter & El Born
The medieval centre with 2,000-year-old Roman walls and the Barcelona Cathedral — then El Born for the emerging natural wine bar and tapas scene.
04
La Boqueria Market at 8am
The famous covered market before the tourist crush — pintxos, jamón ibérico, fresh seafood and Catalan cheeses from stalls that have been here for 180 years.
05
Barceloneta Beach in Shoulder Season
The city beach in May or September — warm without the summer crush. The sunset from Barceloneta looking back at the city skyline is spectacular.
06
El Celler de Can Roca Day Trip to Girona
The three Michelin-star restaurant 45 minutes north by train — consistently among the world's top three. Book 11 months ahead; monthly cancellations released online.
// Areas & Neighbourhoods

Where to base yourself

Eixample
Gaudí buildings, best tapas, local
The modernista grid neighbourhood — all three major Gaudí buildings, the best tapas bars on Carrer del Consell de Cent and a more local feel than the Gothic Quarter.
Gothic Quarter & El Born
History, medieval streets, wine bars
The medieval heart is beautiful but tourist-heavy. El Born (Ribera) is more residential and better for restaurants and nightlife.
Gràcia
Local neighbourhood, cafes
The independent village-within-the-city — bohemian, local, the best neighbourhood for long lazy lunches. Plaça del Sol at sunset.

// When to Go

Season guide

May–Jun: Best time — warm beach weather before the summer crush, uncrowded major attractions. Sep–Oct: Excellent — summer heat without peak crowds. October particularly good. Jul–Aug: Extremely hot and crowded. Accommodation prices double. Las Ramblas is impenetrable. Nov–Mar: Quiet, mild (15–18°C), Sagrada Família without queues.

// Need to Know

Before you go

🎫

Book Sagrada Família, Park Güell and Casa Batlló well ahead — they sell out in high season.

👜

Las Ramblas is the most pickpocketed street in Europe. Walk it to reach the Gothic Quarter but don't linger.

🍽️

The menú del día is how locals eat — typically £10–14 for three courses with wine. Any non-tourist restaurant will have one.

🚇

Barcelona metro is excellent — buy the T-Casual (10-trip card) at any station.

🗣️

Catalan culture is genuinely distinct from Spanish. A 'bon dia' or 'gràcies' is noticed and appreciated.
