Bogotá
Colombia

Street art, emerald markets, extraordinary coffee culture, and a city reinventing itself before your eyes.

Americas · South America

Why visit Bogotá?

Bogotá has transformed beyond recognition in the past 20 years — from one of the world's most dangerous cities to one of Latin America's most vibrant and culturally rich capitals. The change is real and dramatic. La Candelaria's colonial streets, the world's finest collection of pre-Columbian gold art, the street art of La Perseverancia, and a coffee culture that's among the best on earth — Bogotá is the gateway to a Colombia that rewards the curious traveller.

Know Before You Go

Where to Base Yourself in Bogotá

Bogotá isn't one-size-fits-all. Each area has a completely different character — here's how to choose.

🏛️

La Candelaria

Best for: History lovers, art enthusiasts, those wanting to understand Colombia's past

The historic colonial heart of the city — where Bogotá was founded in 1538. The Plaza de Bolívar, the Gold Museum (the finest pre-Columbian collection in the world — 55,000 pieces), the Botero Museum (Fernando Botero donated 123 of his own works plus his personal collection to the city for free public access), and the Palacio de Nariño (presidential palace). Vibrant student area by day.

🎵

Chapinero & Zona Rosa

Best for: Foodies, nightlife lovers, those wanting modern Bogotá

The city's most dynamic neighbourhood for food, nightlife and LGBTQ+ culture. Zona Rosa (also called Zona T) is the upmarket bar and restaurant district. Chapinero Alto has the best independent restaurants and coffee shops. The Parque de la 93 area is where the city's professionals congregate on weekend evenings. Salsa clubs, jazz venues and everything in between.

🌿

Usaquén

Best for: Those wanting a relaxed day, shoppers, brunch enthusiasts, Sunday visitors

A colonial village that Bogotá grew around and absorbed — it retains its separate identity as a neighbourhood of cobblestone streets, antique shops, brunch spots and the best Sunday flea market in the city. The Usaquén district feels like a different pace entirely from downtown Bogotá — relaxed, prosperous and with excellent people-watching from its many café terraces.

⛪

Cerro Monserrate

Best for: Photographers, those wanting panoramic city views, spiritual travellers

The mountain that rises to 3,152m directly above the city — accessible by cable car or funicular from the centre, or by a steep 1.5-hour hiking trail. The white basilica at the top is a significant pilgrimage site. The views over the urban expanse of Bogotá (which stretches 30km from north to south) are extraordinary, especially at dusk as the city's lights come on.

☕

Coffee Region (Zona Cafetera)

Best for: Coffee lovers, those wanting Colombian culture and scenery, day trippers from Bogotá

Three hours from Bogotá by bus, the coffee-growing region (Armenia, Salento, Manizales) is where Colombia's finest coffee is grown and where the traditional bahareque architecture is most perfectly preserved. Salento village and the Cocora Valley (towering wax palm trees, Colombia's national tree) are the highlights. The coffee farm tours (finca visits) give proper insight into the production process.

Insider Knowledge

5 Things to Know Before You Go to Bogotá

  • →

    Bogotá sits at 2,600m — acclimatise for 24 hours before doing anything physically demanding

  • →

    The TransMilenio bus rapid transit system covers most of the city cheaply — a day pass costs around £1.50

  • →

    Always take Uber or inDrive rather than hailing taxis on the street — the app-based services are safer and cheaper

  • →

    Ajiaco (chicken, potato and corn soup) is Bogotá's signature dish — order it in any local restaurant for the definitive Bogotá food experience

  • →

    The Luis Ángel Arango library has free wifi, excellent café facilities and is a genuine cultural institution — a good place to spend a rainy afternoon
