// Oceania & Pacific

Oceania
& the Pacific

The world's most remote continent and the most spectacular — from the Great Barrier Reef to the Southern Alps.

Australia and New Zealand between them contain some of the most extraordinary landscapes on earth. The Great Barrier Reef is the largest living structure visible from space. The Southern Alps of New Zealand look like the Andes dropped into the Pacific. Queenstown invented the concept of adventure tourism. The red centre of Australia around Uluru is one of the most spiritually powerful places in the world. Both countries are expensive by backpacker standards — £55–90/day is realistic — but the working holiday visa (both countries offer them to most under-30 Western nationals) can extend a trip almost indefinitely.

// When to Go

Season guide

Australia: The seasons are reversed from the Northern Hemisphere. December–February is summer — excellent in the south (Sydney, Melbourne), extreme in the north (Darwin, Queensland). June–August is winter in the south but perfect in Queensland and the Northern Territory. New Zealand: December–March for hiking and South Island scenery. June–October for skiing in the Southern Alps. Spring (September–November) and Autumn (March–May) are underrated for mild weather and fewer crowds. Fiji: May–October is the dry season — reliable sunshine and calm seas.

// Need to Know

Before you go

ℹ️

Both Australia and New Zealand offer Working Holiday Visas for most nationalities under 30 — this turns a two-week holiday into a year-long adventure.

ℹ️

Australia is vast — flying between states is the only realistic option for covering ground. A Sydney–Melbourne–Cairns–Uluru circuit is 4+ weeks minimum.

ℹ️

The Great Barrier Reef is best accessed from Cairns. Dive certification done in Cairns (4 days, ~£300) will serve you for life.

ℹ️

New Zealand's South Island has genuinely world-class hiking. The Milford Track, Routeburn Track and Kepler Track all require DOC permits booked months ahead.

ℹ️

Driving in Australia requires international experience — distances are enormous, roads are often straight, and wildlife crossings at dusk are genuinely hazardous.
