Georgia

Ancient monasteries, Caucasus mountain passes, and wine older than anywhere else on earth.

Europe · Caucasus

Why visit Georgia?

Georgia (the country) is one of Europe's most underrated travel destinations — a place where ancient cave monasteries cling to mountain cliffs, where wine has been made in clay qvevri vessels for 8,000 years, where the Caucasus mountains provide some of the continent's most dramatic scenery, and where the hospitality is almost overwhelming in its generosity. Tbilisi's old town is one of the most photogenic cities in Europe. It's very cheap, very safe, and very welcoming.

Where to Stay

Base Yourself in Georgia

Georgia isn't one-size-fits-all. Each area has a completely different character — here's how to choose.

🏘️

Tbilisi

Best for: Everyone — Tbilisi is unmissable

One of the most characterful capital cities in Europe — a tangle of Ottoman-era balconied houses in the old town, an extraordinary art nouveau district, a Soviet modernist television tower, a Persian bath district (Abanotubani) where you can rent a sulphur bath private room for £15/hour, and a food and wine scene that's been attracting international chefs for a decade. Walk the old town slowly.

⛰️

Kazbegi (Stepantsminda)

Best for: Hikers, photographers, those wanting dramatic mountain scenery close to the capital

The mountain town at the foot of Mount Kazbek (5,047m) — a 2.5-hour drive from Tbilisi through increasingly dramatic scenery. The Gergeti Trinity Church on its hilltop above the town (with Kazbek behind it) is one of the iconic images of the Caucasus. Hiking to the church takes 2 hours. The serious trekkers go higher — the base camp for Kazbek summit attempts is a day's hike from town.

🍷

Kakheti (Wine Region)

Best for: Wine lovers, those interested in food culture, couples

Georgia invented wine — amphorae containing wine residue found here date to 6,000 BCE. The Kakheti region in the east is where most Georgian wine is produced, from the Rkatsiteli and Saperavi grapes that are almost unknown outside the country. Signagi is the prettiest town (completely restored historic centre, views over the Alazani valley). Winery visits are cheap and generous with the pours.

⛪

Mtskheta

Best for: History lovers, religious culture enthusiasts, those wanting to understand Georgia's significance

Georgia's ancient capital, 20km from Tbilisi — the spiritual heart of the country. The Svetitskhoveli Cathedral (dating to the 5th century AD, built on what Georgians believe is one of the most sacred spots on earth) and the Jvari Monastery on the hill above (where two rivers converge) are essential. The whole town is UNESCO-listed and takes a comfortable half day from Tbilisi.

🗼

Svaneti

Best for: Adventurous travellers, hikers, photographers, those seeking remote mountain culture

The most remote and dramatic region of Georgia — a highland valley in the northwest where medieval defensive towers built by families still stand in every village. The UNESCO-listed village of Ushguli (one of the highest continuously inhabited settlements in Europe) has 13th-century towers against a backdrop of the Caucasus' highest peaks. The drive or flight from Tbilisi is an experience in itself.

Insider Knowledge

5 Things to Know About Georgia

  • →

    Georgians will not let you pay for food or wine in their home — if invited for dinner, bring wine and accept that you will not go hungry

  • →

    The marshrutka (shared minibus) network connects virtually everywhere and costs almost nothing — £3–8 for most intercity routes

  • →

    Georgian bread (shoti) baked in a tone clay oven is genuinely one of the world's great breads — eat it at every opportunity

  • →

    Natural wine produced in qvevri clay vessels (amber wine) is extraordinary — completely different from anything produced in Western winemaking

  • →

    Visa-free for most nationalities and the lari is very weak against sterling — budget £25–35/day including accommodation, food and transport
