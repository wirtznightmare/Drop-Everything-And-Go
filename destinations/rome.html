// Western Europe · Italy

Rome

The Eternal City — 3,000 years of history that you can walk through in an afternoon.

💰 £60–£100/day 🗓️ Best: Apr–Jun & Sep–Oct 🛂 No visa (Schengen, 90 days) 💬 Italian (English in tourist areas)

Living history, every corner

Rome works through layering. A 2,000-year-old Roman temple becomes a church becomes a piazza with a Renaissance fountain. You turn a corner and a ruined amphitheatre fills your entire field of vision. You eat carbonara in a restaurant that has been making the same recipe since 1948. This is not a museum — it is a living city where all of Western civilisation is simply the backdrop to daily life.

The key to Rome is booking ahead and getting up early. The Colosseum sells out days in advance. The Vatican queue without pre-booking can be 2 hours. The Spanish Steps at 7am are completely empty. Trastevere on a Tuesday evening is the best version of Rome.

// Top Experiences

What to actually do

01
Colosseum Underground & Arena Floor
Book online for the underground option — the animal pens and gladiator corridors beneath the arena floor are extraordinary. Arrive at 9am opening.
02
Vatican Museums & Sistine Chapel Early Entry
The 8am first slot with a guide — the Raphael Rooms and Sistine Chapel ceiling before the coaches arrive. St Peter's and the dome are separate (free entry, long queue).
03
Pantheon at Opening — 9am
The best-preserved ancient building in the world — the 2,000-year-old concrete dome with its oculus open to the sky. Free entry, no booking needed. Go at 9am opening.
04
Trastevere Evening Food Walk
The medieval neighbourhood across the river — no big monuments, just cobblestones, ivy walls and the most authentic restaurants in Rome. Via della Scala for dinner.
05
Borghese Gallery — Bernini Sculptures
The most spectacular baroque sculpture collection in existence — Bernini's Apollo and Daphne alone is reason to visit Rome. Book 2 weeks ahead (only 360 visitors per 2-hour slot).
06
Campo de'Fiori Market at 7am
The daily food and flower market before the tourist crush — then walk to Campo Sant'Angelo for the best cornetto e caffè in Rome.
// Areas & Neighbourhoods

Where to base yourself

Centro Storico
Maximum sightseeing, central
The Pantheon, Piazza Navona and Campo de'Fiori — the postcard Rome. Beautiful and walkable. Expensive to stay but worth it for location.
Trastevere
Food, bars, neighbourhood feel
The most atmospheric neighbourhood — medieval lanes, ivy walls, the best restaurants. Further from major sights but 20 min walk from everything.
Testaccio
Authentic food, local market
The working-class neighbourhood with Rome's best food market and some of the finest traditional restaurants — cacio e pepe and coda alla vaccinara territory.

// When to Go

Season guide

Apr–Jun: The best window — warm, long days, pre-peak crowds. May is arguably perfect. Sep–Oct: Excellent — summer heat drops, warmth remains, golden light. Jul–Aug: Very hot (35–38°C), extremely crowded, hotels double in price. Many Romans leave. Nov–Mar: Quieter, cooler, museum queues short — Christmas is genuinely beautiful in Rome.

// Need to Know

Before you go

🎫

Book Colosseum, Vatican Museums and Borghese Gallery at least 2 weeks ahead — the queues without pre-booking are genuinely awful.

🍝

The menù del giorno (set lunch menu) in non-tourist trattorie is typically £10–15 for three courses with wine. Ask if it's not posted outside.

🚇

The Roma Pass (48hr, £25) includes free metro travel and one free museum — worth it if using public transport.

👜

Pickpockets active around the Colosseum, Vatican and major transport hubs. Use cross-body bags.

⛪

Many churches are free — Santa Maria Maggiore, San Giovanni in Laterano and Santa Maria Sopra Minerva contain extraordinary art at no cost.
