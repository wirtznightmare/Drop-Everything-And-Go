// East Asia · Japan

Kyoto

The soul of Japan — 1,600 Buddhist temples, 400 Shinto shrines and the last geisha districts.

💰 £60–£100/day 🗓️ Best: Mar–May & Oct–Nov 🛂 No visa (up to 90 days) 💬 Japanese

Ancient Japan, still standing

Kyoto was Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years and the weight of that history is felt everywhere — in every temple garden, every stone lantern path, every wooden machiya townhouse. While Tokyo is Japan's future, Kyoto is its past, and it is extraordinary.

The key is timing and early starts. Fushimi Inari's 10,000 torii gates at 6am before any other tourists. The Arashiyama bamboo grove in the first light. The Gion geisha district at dusk when the lanterns come on. Kyoto at these moments is completely unlike any other city in the world.

// Quick Facts

💰
Daily Budget
£60–100. Budget hostels from £18/night. Temple entry £5–8 each.
🗓️
Best Time
Mar–May (cherry blossom) and Oct–Nov (foliage). Book 6+ months ahead for both.
🚆
Getting There
Bullet train from Tokyo (2h20, JR Pass accepted). 75 min from Osaka.
💴
Currency
Japanese Yen. Most temples cash-only.
📱
Tip
IC card (Suica/Icoca) works on all buses and trains — load at the station.
// Top Experiences

What to actually do

01
Fushimi Inari Before 7am
10,000 vermilion torii gates winding through cedar forest. Arrive at opening for complete solitude — it is a completely different place before the crowds.
02
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove
The famous grove is genuinely beautiful at 6am. Stay for Tenryu-ji garden and the preserved Saga-Toriimoto street along the old pilgrimage route.
03
Gion Evening Walk
The geisha district at dusk — lantern-lit machiya houses and if lucky a geiko or maiko in full dress heading to an engagement. Hanamikoji Street after 6pm.
04
Kinkaku-ji Golden Pavilion
The gold-leaf Zen temple reflected in the mirror pond. Genuinely stunning despite the crowds — arrive at opening for the best light and shortest queues.
05
Traditional Tea Ceremony
A formal matcha ceremony in a 100-year-old townhouse — the ritual teaches more about Japanese culture in one hour than a week of sightseeing.
06
Philosopher's Path in Season
The canal path lined with cherry trees — extraordinary in April. Equally beautiful in November when the maples turn crimson along the water.
// Areas & Neighbourhoods

Where to base yourself

Gion & Higashiyama
Geisha culture, preserved streets
The old city — preserved machiya houses, the best temples and the geisha district. Stay here for the most atmospheric experience.
Arashiyama
Bamboo grove, Zen temples
The western temple district — best visited early morning before 8am. The monkey park above the valley has spectacular views over Kyoto.
Fushimi
Torii gates, sake breweries
The southern district with Fushimi Inari and the sake brewery area — an easy half-day and far less crowded than the centre.

// When to Go

Season guide

Mar–Apr: Cherry blossom — the most beautiful time but book 6+ months ahead for accommodation and the most popular temples. Oct–Nov: Autumn foliage — equally spectacular, slightly less crowded. Jun: Rainy season but atmospheric. Jul–Aug: Hot and very crowded. Dec–Feb: Cold but quiet — occasional snow on the temples is extraordinary.

// Need to Know

Before you go

🚲

Kyoto is best explored by bicycle — rentals from £5/day. The bus network is also excellent with the Kyoto Bus Pass.

🏨

Book ryokan months ahead for cherry blossom and autumn foliage. Prices triple and rooms disappear in October.

📷

Photography inside temples is often restricted — look for signs. Photographing geisha without permission is considered very rude.

💴

Many shrines and temples are cash-only. Always carry yen notes in small denominations.

🎫

The 2-day Arashiyama Pass covers key buses and the scenic Sagano railway — worth buying at Kyoto station.
